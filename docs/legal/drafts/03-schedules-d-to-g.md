# Schedule D — Confidentiality and Data Handling

## Part A — Confidentiality

### D1. Permitted use

A receiving party may use Confidential Information only to perform or receive the services, assess a project, operate the agreed collaboration or exercise rights under the Master Agreement. It must not publish, sell, disclose, copy for unrelated use or exploit Confidential Information without written permission. Access must be limited to people who need it and are subject to appropriate confidentiality obligations.

### D2. Security and incident reporting

Each party must use reasonable safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information. The Contributor must not share passwords, use another person's account, place unreleased files in publicly accessible storage or send private material to an unauthorised recipient.

A party discovering suspected loss, unauthorised access or disclosure must notify the other without undue delay and, where possible, within 24 hours of becoming aware. The notice should describe what happened, what information may be affected and what steps have been taken. The parties must cooperate reasonably to contain and investigate the incident, preserve relevant evidence and meet legal notification duties.

### D3. Exceptions and lawful disclosures

Confidential Information does not include information the receiving party can show became public without breach, was lawfully known before disclosure, was independently developed without using it, was lawfully received from an unrestricted third party, or must be disclosed by law or court order.

Nothing prevents a party from obtaining legal, tax, medical or professional advice; reporting suspected crime or unlawful conduct; making a protected disclosure; reporting harassment, discrimination, abuse or safety concerns; exercising data-protection, employment, consumer or other statutory rights; or retaining evidence reasonably required for a complaint, legal advice or proceedings.

Where disclosure is compelled by law, the receiving party must, where lawful, give advance notice and disclose only what is required.

### D4. Duration and return

Confidentiality obligations apply during the Agreement and for five years after it ends. Trade secrets and personal data remain protected for as long as they retain that status or the law requires. On request or termination, the receiving party must return or securely delete information no longer needed, except lawful records, dispute evidence or secure backups that cannot reasonably be isolated for immediate deletion. Any retained copy remains protected.

The Processor must keep reasonable records and must not use personal data for its own advertising, profiling, unrelated analytics or generative AI training.

## G2. Permission and limits

The Contributor authorises the Operator and named transcription provider to process the identified audio solely to convert spoken words into written text for the selected purpose. Permission includes temporary technical copying and processing reasonably necessary to create, review, correct, store and display the transcript in the agreed NightJar workflow.

It does not include:
(a) training, fine-tuning or improving a generative AI or speech model;
(b) creating synthetic voices, voice clones or imitations;
(c) generating new creative content based on the Contributor's voice or performance;
(d) using the audio or transcript for unrelated advertising; or
(e) providing the material to another provider except the named provider and approved technical sub-processors needed for transcription.

## G3. Human review

Automated transcription may contain errors. The Operator or Contributor may review and correct the transcript before publication. It must not be represented as verbatim or legally certified unless checked to the required standard. The Contributor may request correction of a material error.
