# NIGHTJAR — INTERNAL CREATOR CONTRACT AUDIT REPORT

**Founder review version 0.2 — 8 November 2026**  
**Status: READY FOR MEG AND MIKE TO REVIEW; NOT READY FOR SIGNATURE OR PUBLICATION**

This report records the drafting and legal-risk audit of the NightJar creator contract pack. It is an internal review document, not part of any contract and not a legal opinion or certificate of compliance.

## 1. Overall conclusion

The original idea of one Master Contract supported by sub-contracts is sound, provided the sub-documents are structured as selected schedules and separate work-specific rights instruments rather than unrelated documents that can contradict each other.

The current draft pack now has a coherent structure:
- one Master Contributor Agreement for the common legal relationship;
- selected schedules for voluntary work, commissions, Creator Pool, confidentiality/data processing, NightJar Originals and transcription;
- a separate direct creator-to-creator agreement;
- separate performer and moral-rights instruments;
- role-specific project scopes;
- a signing and recordkeeping procedure; and
- a plain-English glossary and founder decision register.

I consider this a substantially complete **founder-review draft pack**. I do not consider it legally approved for creators to sign. The remaining blockers are specific and listed in section 4. No one should be asked to sign until those matters are decided and the final party details and work-specific information are completed.

## 3. Material legal issues addressed

### Copyright and future copyright

The paid-commission schedule does not rely on an upload or payment record alone. It uses a work-specific written assignment and provides that the transfer takes effect only after full payment. The statutory framework requires a copyright assignment to be written and signed by or on behalf of the assignor; separate provisions deal with future copyright. Each person who owns relevant rights must sign or validly authorise the transfer.

### Performers and recordings

The pack now treats the script, sound recording and recorded performance as distinct rights. It includes a separate performer release before recording/use and a separate signed assignment where the performer intends to transfer their performer property rights. A writer's or producer's assignment cannot transfer a performer's rights.

### Moral rights

Moral rights are not assigned with copyright. The new optional instrument allows a specific consent, a narrow waiver or no waiver. A waiver is not included as a blanket term in the Master Agreement.

### Employment status

The draft does not declare all contributors to be self-employed. It records that the legal status depends on the real working relationship. This is important where a creator is required to work personally, is controlled in their hours or methods, or becomes economically dependent on NightJar.

### Personal data

The Data Processing Addendum is conditional. It should be used only where the contributor actually processes personal data on the Operator's behalf. It includes the principal UK GDPR Article 28 processor requirements and does not replace the public Privacy Notice, lawful-basis assessment or a data-protection complaints process.

### Electronic signatures

The signing protocol requires evidence of the signer's intention to authenticate the exact document. For copyright/performer assignments and moral-rights waivers, use a signing workflow designed to capture the signature and preserve the final signed document and audit evidence. Do not treat a generic platform checkbox as a substitute.

### Liability and fairness

The Master Agreement uses a mutual liability framework with a proposed general cap and specific exceptions for unpaid sums, fraud, deliberate IP misuse, deliberate confidentiality/data misuse and liabilities that cannot lawfully be limited. The cap is a drafting proposal, not a guaranteed enforceable figure. Where the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 applies, reasonableness must be considered in the context of the contract and the parties.

## 4. Must resolve before signature

These matters cannot responsibly be filled in by guesswork:

1. **Legal contracting party:** identify the exact legal person who will contract with creators. The name “NightJar Audio” alone is insufficient. Confirm whether the founders' actual arrangements amount to an ordinary partnership or something else. Ordinary partners may have personal responsibility for business obligations.
2. **Creator Pool eligibility:** complete the role-specific table. The application should not be allowed to award a legal entitlement based on undefined or changing rules.
3. **Unallocated Creator Pool on closure:** decide how the carried-forward balance will be treated if NightJar closes permanently or stops operating.
4. **Creator Pool payment timetable:** approve or change the proposed statement within 20 days after month end and payment within 30 days after month end.
5. **Tax and VAT:** accountant to verify subscription VAT treatment, creator tax/withholding obligations, and founder payments.
6. **Actual rights chain:** each writer, artist, sound-recording producer and performer who owns rights must sign the appropriate instrument.
7. **NightJar Originals:** no exclusive work can be approved until all terms are complete and signed.
8. **Data processor activities:** complete Schedule D only for contributors whose actual work involves personal data processing on NightJar's behalf.
9. **Transcription service:** no audio is to be sent to a provider until its terms/configuration and no-training position have been verified.
10. **Execution process:** choose and test the signing and document-retention process before using any of the rights instruments.
11. **Public legal pages:** reconcile the new creator contracts with the existing platform Terms and Professional & Content Terms. Add the current Information Commission complaint route and ensure privacy and subscription information is complete.
12. **Liability cap:** Meg and Mike should consider whether the proposed £1,000/fees cap is proportionate for the types of work and risks NightJar expects.

## 5. Operating model consistency checks

The draft reflects the following current operating principles:
- voluntary submissions remain creator-owned under a limited, non-exclusive platform licence;
- commissioned work is assigned to NightJar only through a signed, work-specific rights instrument and after full payment;
- 50% of Eligible Net Subscription Revenue is allocated to the Creator Pool;
- each eligible person receives one equal share, regardless of the number of roles;
- Meg opts out of the Creator Pool; her owner compensation is separate;
- creators pay one another directly; NightJar does not hold or route creator-to-creator funds;
- NightJar Originals require separate express agreement;
- speech-to-text is optional and does not authorise model training or voice cloning; and
- NightJar's intended creator payout route is direct bank transfer through its nominated Monzo account.

The existing Compensation Control Centre hardcodes a Stripe provider label for revenue settlement entries. That appears to describe incoming subscription settlements, not necessarily creator payouts. The confirmed creator payout route is Monzo. Confirm the actual subscription collection route before making a production finance-code change. The contracts do not assume Stripe fees.
