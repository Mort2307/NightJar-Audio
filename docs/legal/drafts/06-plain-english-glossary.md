# NIGHTJAR CONTRACTS — PLAIN-ENGLISH GLOSSARY

**Internal drafting aid.** Where a defined term appears in a signed contract, the contract definition takes priority over this general explanation.

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| Assignment | A transfer of ownership of a right, such as copyright or a performer's property right, from one person to another. It is more than permission to use something. |
| Copyright | Legal rights that protect original creative work, such as writing, music, artwork, films and sound recordings. Different people may own different copyrights in one production. |
| Future copyright | Copyright in a work that has not yet been created. An agreement can deal with it in advance only if the statutory requirements are met and the work is clearly identified. |
| Exclusive licence | Permission for one party to use specified rights to the exclusion of everyone else, potentially including the owner. The scope, territory, duration and permitted uses must be clear. |
| Non-exclusive licence | Permission to use specified rights while the owner can also use the work and grant permission to others. |
| Royalty-free | No continuing royalty is payable for the licensed use unless the agreement separately says otherwise. It does not automatically mean the licence is unlimited. |
| Worldwide | The permission or right applies in all countries. |
| Perpetual | No fixed end date. It does not remove legal limits or rights that cannot lawfully be transferred. |
| Sublicence | Permission for the recipient of a licence to allow another person or company to use the rights within the agreed scope. |
| Moral rights | Certain personal rights of an author, including rights connected with being identified as the author and objecting to derogatory treatment of a work. They are separate from copyright ownership and cannot be assigned. |
| Moral-rights waiver | A signed written document in which an author gives up specified moral rights to a defined extent. A waiver should not be broader than needed. |
| Consent | Permission for a particular act or use. Consent to one recording or edit does not automatically authorise unrelated or additional uses. |
| Performer's rights | Legal rights held by a performer in relation to their performance, including control over certain recording and use of that performance. These are separate from copyright in the script or sound recording. |
| Performer's property rights | The economic rights a performer may own in their performance. They can be assigned, but the assignment must be in writing and signed by or on behalf of the performer. |
| Sound-recording producer | The person who undertakes the arrangements necessary for making a sound recording. The producer may own copyright in the sound recording, separately from the scriptwriter's copyright and the performer's rights. |
| Pre-existing material | Work, tools, templates, methods or assets a creator already owned or developed before the specific commission. |
| Third-party material | Material owned or controlled by someone other than the contracting parties, such as stock images, music, samples, fonts or another person's writing. |
| Statement of Work | A project-specific document stating exactly what is being commissioned, the fee, delivery dates, revisions, rights arrangement and other project details. |
| Voluntary Contribution | Work a creator chooses to submit without a separate commission fee. Under Schedule A, the creator keeps ownership and grants NightJar a limited platform licence. |
| Commissioned Work | Work NightJar specifically orders and pays for under a signed Statement of Work. |
| Creator Pool | The agreed share of eligible subscription revenue set aside for qualifying creators under Schedule C. |
| Eligible Net Subscription Revenue | Subscription receipts actually received, after only the agreed direct deductions such as VAT, refunds, chargebacks and evidenced third-party processing fees. |
| Accrued entitlement | Money or another right already earned under the agreement, even if it has not yet been paid. |
| Controller | A person or organisation that decides why and how personal data is used. |
| Processor | A person or company that handles personal data only on another organisation's instructions. |
| Sub-processor | A supplier used by a processor to help handle personal data on the controller's behalf. |
| Personal data | Information that identifies a living person or can reasonably be used to identify them, directly or indirectly. |
| Biometric data | Personal data resulting from specific technical processing of physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics that allows or confirms unique identification. An ordinary voice recording is not automatically biometric data simply because it contains a voice. |
| Data breach | A security incident that leads to personal data being lost, changed, disclosed or accessed without authorisation. |
| Indemnity | A promise to compensate another party for specified losses or claims. It can create substantial financial exposure and must be narrowly drafted. |
| Liability cap | An agreed maximum amount a party may have to pay for certain claims. It must not attempt to limit liability that the law says cannot be limited. |
| Material breach | A serious failure to comply with an important contractual obligation. |
| Long-stop date | A final deadline after which a specified consequence applies, such as a right to end an exclusivity arrangement if a release has not happened. |
| VAT | Value Added Tax, a tax that may apply to certain supplies. Whether it applies depends on the supplier, supply and tax rules. |
| Working Day | As defined in the contract; in this draft it excludes Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays in England and Wales. |

## Drafting rule

If a contract uses a technical term not listed here, either define it in that contract or replace it with ordinary language. Never rely on a creator knowing what a legal phrase means.
