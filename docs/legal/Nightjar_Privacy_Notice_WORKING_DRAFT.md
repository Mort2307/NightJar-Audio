## 1. Who this notice covers

This notice covers visitors, listeners, applicants, voice actors, writers, audio editors, artists, collaborators and people who contact Nightjar for help. It applies to personal data processed by the Nightjar operator. Independent age-assurance, payment or identity providers may also act as separate controllers and provide their own notices for their services.

## 2. Categories of data used

**Account and identity:** email address, account identifier, display name, account role, login/authentication metadata, lawful-access status, security and verification events.

**Optional public profiles:** handle, pronouns (if provided), introductions, avatar, role-specific details, portfolio, credits, public tags, social links and discoverability settings.

**Professional access and content:** applications, identity/eligibility attestations, rights declarations, uploaded and revised work, private scripts, recordings, work permissions, agreements, credits, collaboration requests, private professional messages and moderation decisions.

**Audio listening and preferences:** subscriptions, entitlements, saved releases, follows, listening progress, recommendations and stated interests. Because Nightjar concerns adult content, even seemingly ordinary interests and listening patterns could sometimes reveal information about sex life or sexual orientation; see section 5.

**Age assurance:** the outcome of verification, verification reference, policy/version, dates and any minimal audit evidence necessary to prove lawful access. We should not receive or retain full ID images or biometric templates unless this is genuinely necessary, contractually authorised and lawfully assessed.

**Billing and gifts (if activated):** payment-provider customer and transaction references, subscription status, billing periods, refunds, gift credits, payout statements, eligible beneficiary records, accounting and fraud events. Complete payment card numbers should be handled by the approved hosted processor rather than stored by Nightjar.

**Safety and technical records:** reports, complaints, decisions and appeals, platform-authentication/security events, limited diagnostic and device data, push messaging tokens, support correspondence and legal disclosures.

## 3. Why the data is processed and proposed Article 6 lawful bases

**Provide accounts, requested listening and professional tools — contract:** registration, entitlement checks, accounts, content delivery, permitted private collaboration and necessary support.

**Protect the service and prevent fraud and abuse — legitimate interests or legal obligation as appropriate:** authentication security, access logging, investigating genuine reports, chargebacks, rights protection and proportionate defensive monitoring. A legitimate-interests assessment must be documented for relevant activities.

**Meet legal or regulatory duties — legal obligation:** applicable age-assurance measures, content-related legal duties, statutory accounting, lawful authority requests and legal record preservation, to the extent the precise duty applies to Nightjar.

**Take payment and provide purchased benefits — contract / legal obligation:** processor integration, renewal events, payment reconciliation, tax and legal reporting when billing is enabled.

**Send marketing messages or use non-essential trackers — consent or another expressly assessed applicable basis:** promotional emails, certain cookies and similar technologies. Marketing consent must be separately offered and withdrawable; account or safety notices are not marketing.

**Public discoverability and certain optional profile features — contract or consent depending on the operation:** profile publication must have clear controls. Nightjar must document the actual basis for each optional feature rather than assume consent through its general Terms.

## 5. Sensitive information and adult preferences

Listening choices, adult-content tags, comments or private conversations may reveal or permit inferences about sexual orientation, sex life or other special-category information. Where Nightjar processes Article 9 special-category data, it must identify and document both an Article 6 lawful basis **and** a valid Article 9 condition before processing it, with appropriate minimisation, access controls and a DPIA where required. Explicit consent is one potential condition but is not inferred from the general acceptance of Terms or Privacy Notice.

Nightjar should avoid collecting special-category traits it does not need; make optional preferences genuinely optional, and avoid publishing a person's sensitive listening behaviour, interests or verification status to other users.

## 6. Age verification and identity documents

Where required, Nightjar will use a production age-assurance measure assessed against the applicable Ofcom standard. The verification provider may receive identity or age-related inputs directly. Nightjar's preference is to retain a minimal over-18 verification outcome and audit token rather than raw identity documents, unless a documented lawful reason requires additional information.

Users will receive a provider-specific explanation before verification, including controller relationships, data collected, sharing, retention and how to exercise rights. Nightjar must confirm what the selected provider actually stores before releasing the final notice.

Rights are subject to statutory limits and genuine exemptions, for example where information must be retained for legal claims or another lawful duty.

Send requests to [WORKING PRIVACY EMAIL]. We may need proportionate identity checks before releasing data. We will handle requests within the applicable statutory deadline, explaining any extension or lawful refusal as required.

## 12. Cookies, analytics and direct marketing

Necessary security, sign-in or service cookies may operate where legally permitted. Non-essential analytics, advertising and similar storage/access technologies will not be enabled without the required information and consent under UK privacy/e-communications rules. The final Cookie Notice must name actual technology, purpose, retention and user controls rather than use generic language.

Account, billing and safety messages necessary to operate the service are not automatically marketing. Marketing preferences must be separate, clear and easy to change; consent or other lawful permission must be demonstrated before sending promotional messages.

Updates will carry an effective date and, for materially new purposes or substantial changes affecting individuals, appropriate direct notice and any newly required choice. A new Privacy Notice version is not itself consent to sensitive profiling or marketing.
